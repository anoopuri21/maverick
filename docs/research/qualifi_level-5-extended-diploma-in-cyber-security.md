## 3. Qualification Overview — the richest bespoke copy in the Qualifi set

Substance, closely following the source:

- The rationale is a career path for learners developing core capabilities in the cyber
  security and risk management sector, so they build the cyber security skills
  organisations need, and to provide a pathway via learner credits and potential direct
  entry into related Level 6 cyber security and/or risk management degree programmes.
- Qualifi calls it **"potentially unique because it is about 'cyber security' as a
  workplace management discipline"** — a **semi-technical** qualification examining
  technical measures alongside the management, project management and leadership skills
  needed to organise internal and external responses to major incidents or threats.
- It promises solid business management and leadership education — **including disaster
  management, project management and business continuity management** — set in the
  digital and cyber threat environment.
- **"The course programme is designed and delivered by a mix of academics from Business
  Schools and Security Management teaching roles."**
- Aimed especially at existing or aspiring executives and leaders responsible for
  business resilience, information security, safe technological innovation, secure
  change management, HR planning and physical risk management.
- **"The course is flexible and online. It is easily able to be blended."** It uses many
  business and public-sector case studies and **"embeds isomorphic learning"**.
  Particularly suited to people already in work, or seeking structured management
  competency in information security, risk management and organisational resilience.

⚠️ **Three handling notes:**
1. **"Potentially unique"** is Qualifi's own hedged marketing. Do not restate it as
   "unique". Describe the management framing factually instead — it genuinely is the
   distinguishing feature.
2. **"The course is flexible and online"** conflicts with the standing fact that Qualifi
   does not teach — delivery is the approved centre's. Treat this as a description of
   how the programme is *designed* to be delivered, and keep the centre caveat.
   → **GAP-SOURCE-QF-23.**
3. **"Isomorphic learning"** is jargon. Translate for a lay reader as learning from how
   failures in one organisation or sector repeat in another, and attribute the term to
   Qualifi rather than explaining it as established pedagogy.

## 4. Units — 11 published under a "Mandatory" heading

1. Cyber Security Threat and Risk
2. Network Security and Data Communications
3. Database Security and Computer Programming
4. Incident Response, Investigations and Forensics
5. Security Strategy: Laws, Policies and Implementation
6. Cyber Security Threats and Risk: Banking and Finance
7. Cyber Wars
8. Cryptography
9. Digital Investigations and Forensics
10. Communications and Incident Management
11. Strategic Leadership

⚠️ **Eleven units against 240 credits**, all marked Mandatory with no optional group.
The heading implies a second group that is not printed. → **GAP-SOURCE-QF-24.**

Observations worth using: a **banking and finance** sector unit is unusual at this
level; forensics appears twice (units 4 and 9, from the Level 4 and Level 5 halves);
Strategic Leadership closing the list matches the management framing of the overview.

## 5. Learning Outcomes (9, verbatim substance)

1. Develop the knowledge, understanding and skills required for success in cyber-security-related employment.
2. **Progress into Level 5 learning within Cyber Security domains.** ← *leftover Level 4 outcome, incongruous on a Level 5 page. Do not reproduce.*
3. Carry out specialist study relevant to individual vocations and environments in which learners work or aim to work, in business and public service sectors.
4. Contribute positively to good practice in business technology and risk management environments by combining the knowledge and skills gained.
5. Develop skills, techniques, personal qualities and attributes essential for successful performance in working life, enabling an immediate positive contribution to employment.
6. Equip individuals with the knowledge, understanding and skills required for success in information-security-related employment.
7. **Enable progression into a university-approved Level 6 Degree award.**
8. Provide specialist study relevant to individual vocations and environments in business and public service sectors.
9. Contribute positively to good and ethical practice in technology and risk management environments through practical and theoretical knowledge and skills.

Outcomes 1/6, 3/8 and 4/9 are near-duplicate pairs — the Level 4 and Level 5 sets
concatenated. **Consolidate when writing; do not pad the `learning` field with the
duplicates.** → GAP-SOURCE-QF-25.

## 6. Entry Requirements (verbatim, four bullets)

- Qualifications at Level 3 and/or;
- Some technical and risk management experience in a computing or security business environment and demonstrate ambition with clear career goals;
- A Level 4 qualification in another discipline and who want to develop their careers in cyber security.
- **Level 4 units must be completed before level 5 units.**

*(sic — the first bullet ends "and/or;" mid-phrase.)*

**No age minimum and no IELTS requirement published**, unlike the IT family and most
other Level 5 awards. Do not import those rules.

The sequencing rule (Level 4 before Level 5) is published on **no other** Qualifi page
seen so far and is genuinely useful to a reader planning their study.

## 9. Notes

- The management-rather-than-hacking framing is the honest hook: this suits the person
  who will have to **answer** for an incident, not the person running the tooling.
- Qualifi runs cyber security at Levels 2, 3, 4, 5 (this one) and 7 (tracker S.No 67),
  the deepest ladder in its catalogue. One specification covers Levels 2 to 5.
- Contrast with S.No 44 (Networking and Cyber Security, 610/3042/9): research that row
  before writing either page, since they overlap in subject and must be differentiated.
