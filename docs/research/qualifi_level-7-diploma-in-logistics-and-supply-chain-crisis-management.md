## 3. ⭐ Qualification Overview — bespoke and genuinely good

Verbatim in substance:
- A qualification for **aspiring strategic leaders within the logistics and supply chain
  sector**.
- It "looks to equip leaders with the tools necessary to **build strategic resilience
  into their supply chains, in a blend of historic models and modern day techniques**".
- Designed to provide the expertise required for a career as a **logistics and supply
  chain leader**, and a sound basis for progression to postgraduate study.
- A closing paragraph hopes centres and learners gain relevant new skills and knowledge,
  encouraging academic and professional development so learners realise their own
  potential and that of organisations across the industry.

⭐ **"Build strategic resilience into their supply chains"** is the sharpest single phrase
on the page and the natural editorial spine. Resilience and crisis — not day-to-day
logistics operations — are what this award is about. That is also the honest framing:
nothing in it covers warehousing, transport planning or procurement operations.

⚠️ The third paragraph addresses centres rather than learners ("We hope that centres and
learners take the opportunity…"). It is awarding-body marketing copy, not qualification
content. Do not quote it.

⚠️ Overview claims "a sound basis for progression to postgraduate study" — consistent
here with the published dissertation route, unlike S.No 58 (QF-52).

## 5. Learning Outcomes (8, verbatim)
1. Apply and make critiques of different approaches and implementation standards with regard to logistics and supply chain operations.
2. Evaluate the role of strategic leadership in supply chain risk management.
3. Review the impact of globalisation on an organisation's extended logistics and supply chain operations.
4. Understand and apply the principles of sustainability in supply chain leadership.
5. Analyse problem-solving techniques that are specific to supply chain crisis management and resilience building.
6. Manage strategic risks and build resilience in a global supply chain.
7. Analyse the impacts of supply chain shocks on business operations.
8. Evaluate resilience measures in the context of differing global supply chain operations.

⚠️⚠️ **Outcomes 1–6 follow exactly the same template as S.No 58 (International OH&S) and
S.No 59 (Process Safety)** — same verbs, same order, same structure, with only the
subject noun swapped:

| # | Template | 58 | 59 | **68** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | "Apply and make critiques of … implementation standards" | OH&S management | process safety | **logistics and supply chain** |
| 2 | "Evaluate the role of strategic leadership in …" | risk management | risk-based process safety | **supply chain risk management** |
| 3 | "Review the impact of … in an organisation's … culture/operations" | globalisation / safety culture | Covid / process safety culture | **globalisation / supply chain operations** |
| 4 | "Understand and apply the principles of sustainability in …" | safety sector | process safety (inherently safer designs) | **supply chain leadership** |
| 5 | "Analyse problem-solving techniques … specific to …" | risk assessment | process safety incidents (HAZOP) | **crisis management and resilience** |
| 6 | "Manage strategic risks in …" | H&S domain | process safety domain | **a global supply chain** |

→ **GAP-STYLE-QF-83.** These three pages must never describe their outcomes in similar
prose. Differentiate on **units** — which are completely distinct — and on audience.
Outcomes 7 and 8 here (supply chain shocks, resilience measures) are unique to this row
and should carry the weight.
