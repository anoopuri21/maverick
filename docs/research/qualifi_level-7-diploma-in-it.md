## 3. Qualification Overview (verbatim)

> "This Diploma in Information Technology (IT) aims to develop learners' ability to solve
> complex applied computing problems in information technology. Learners will learn to
> manage IT projects and find out how information technology affects society. Further, it
> will also create the ability to design, plan and organise technology-based projects
> that are produced on time, to high standards and within budget."

⭐ Short but genuinely on-topic and usable. Three clear ideas: **solving complex applied
computing problems**, **managing IT projects** (on time, to standard, on budget), and
**how information technology affects society**. The project-management emphasis and the
society angle are the differentiators from S.No 66 and 67.

⚠️ The overview calls it "This Diploma in Information Technology (IT)" without the level
or awarding body. Always use the full official title in our content.

## 4. Units (6, published under a "Mandatory" heading)
1. Computer Networks
2. Data Analytics
3. Database Management Systems
4. Management Information Systems
5. Computers and Society
6. Computing Projects

Six units, 120 credits, TQT 1200, no per-unit credits or hours published
→ GAP-SOURCE-QF-08.

⭐ A broad, classic computing curriculum rather than a specialism — networks, data,
databases, information systems, ethics and society, and a project. **Computers and
Society** is unusual and distinctive; it is the only "IT and society" unit in the
tracker. **Computing Projects** indicates substantial applied work.

⚠️ "Mandatory" is published as a heading with no optional list beneath it. Treat all six
as required; do not imply options exist.

⚠️ Unit titles overlap the Level 5 IT family (networks, data, databases). The honest
separation is level and purpose: Level 5 builds the foundations, this sits above them
with management information systems, a project and a societal dimension. Never reuse the
Level 5 pages' prose about these subjects — a proven collision.

## 5. ⚠️⚠️ Learning Outcomes — NONE PUBLISHED

The page provides an "Outcomes" anchor in its own navigation, but **no Learning Outcomes
section exists on the page at all**. This is not a formatting fault or a truncation: the
section is absent between Units and Entry Requirements.

→ **GAP-SOURCE-QF-76.** Precedent exists: several Level 5 pages publish no outcomes, and
the agreed handling applies here unchanged —
- the `learning` block must open with an explicit preamble stating that Qualifi publishes
  no learning outcomes for this qualification and that the list below is **our summary of
  the subject coverage implied by the six published units and the overview**;
- every item must be traceable to a published unit title or an overview sentence;
- nothing may be imported from S.No 66, S.No 67 or the Level 5 IT pages;
- the absence must also be disclosed in the snapshot and in an FAQ.

This is one of only two Level 7 rows where outcomes are unusable — the other being
S.No 56, where three are published but two are not outcomes at all.

## 6. ⭐ Entry Requirements — the most fully specified in the Level 7 set
- Designed to be accessible without artificial barriers that restrict access **and progression**.
- **Entry is through a centre interview.**
- In certain circumstances, **managers with considerable experience but no formal
  qualifications** may be considered, subject to interview and demonstrating the ability
  to cope with the demands of the programme.
- IELTS 6 (or equivalent) where English is not the first language.
- International qualifications checked for appropriate **matriculation** to UK higher
  education postgraduate programmes (⚠️ "matriculation", not the usual "enrolment" —
  wording unique to this page).
- Normally two supporting references, at least one preferably academic.
- Learners must meet **one** of:
  - completion of a **Bachelor's degree in IT**;
  - completion of a **Level 6 qualification in a relevant discipline**;
  - **3 to 4 years' work experience in a related field**.
- **Applicants must be aged 20 or over.**

⭐ Three genuinely distinctive features: the **centre interview**, the explicit
**experience-only route for managers without formal qualifications**, and the **age 20**
minimum — a value used on no other row (others use 16, 18, 19 or 21).

⚠️ "3 to 4 years' work experience" is an odd range; report it exactly as published rather
than rounding to "3 years".
