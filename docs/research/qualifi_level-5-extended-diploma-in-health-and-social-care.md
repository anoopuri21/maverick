## 3. Qualification Overview (verbatim)

> "The Qualification has been created to develop and reward the business health and
> social care workers of today and the future, and to continue to bring recognition
> and professionalism to the health and social care sector. The rationale of the
> programmes is to provide a career path for learners who wish to develop their
> management and care capabilities within the health and social care sector."

Note the stray word "business" in "business health and social care workers" — a
copy-paste artefact from the business-diploma boilerplate. **Do not reproduce it.**
Closely related to the S.No 29 overview; paraphrase independently.

specification, which is why it includes both entry-level study skills and a Research

not state a per-unit credit value.

## 5. Learning Outcomes (9, verbatim)

1. Analysing, synthesising and summarising information critically.
2. The ability to read and use appropriate literature with a full and critical understanding, the ability to think independently and solve problems.
3. Apply subject knowledge and understanding to address familiar and unfamiliar problems.
4. Recognise the moral and ethical issues of health and social care practice and research; appreciating the need for ethical standards and professional codes of conduct.
5. An appreciation of the interdisciplinary nature of health and social care service provision.
6. Capacity to give a clear and accurate account of a subject, assemble arguments in a mature way and engage in debate and dialogue both with specialists and non-specialists.
7. Transferable skills and knowledge which will enable individuals to meet changing needs.
8. Circumstances, whether within their own area of employment, or through promotion to a supervisory or management position, or to adapt to changes in the health/social care environment. *(sic — reads as a continuation of outcome 7; the original sentence is split)*
9. To motivate individuals to progress to further professional development through future study or as part of their chosen career.

These are markedly more academic than the Level 3 outcomes (S.No 29) — critical
analysis, literature, research ethics. That contrast is the honest differentiator.

## 6. Entry Requirements (verbatim substance)

- Approved centres decide on the applicant's ability to complete the programme and
  meet its demands; the initial assessment must consider available learner support.
- Designed to be accessible without artificial barriers.
- **Applicants must be aged 18 or over.**
- "It is envisaged that learners entering the Level 4 Diploma will have a Level 3
  qualification. Learners entering the Level 5 Diploma will have a Level 4
  qualification." → **for this Level 5 award, a Level 4 qualification is envisaged.**
- IELTS 6 (or equivalent) where English is not the first language.
- International qualifications checked for appropriate enrolment.
- Normally two supporting references, at least one preferably academic.

⚠️ The entry text carries the incongruous "UK higher education **postgraduate**
programmes" clause on a Level 5 page. Same artefact seen at Level 3. **Do not repeat it.**

## 7. Career outcomes

**None published.** No destinations, salaries or employment rates. Progression route
is "Final year of an Honours degree" only.

⚠️ **MANDATORY, as at Level 3:** Qualifi says nothing about care-sector registration
or licence to practise. The page must state plainly that this is a knowledge
qualification and confers no professional registration, in the UK or anywhere else.

## 9. Notes

- Progression is to the **final year** of an honours degree — the strongest exit route
  of any Qualifi diploma below Level 6. Published route, not a guaranteed place.
- The specification PDF covers Levels 4 **and** 5 together, which explains the mixed
  unit list. Reader should be told the single document covers both.
- Contrast with S.No 29 (Level 3, 60 credits, TQT 600): this is four times the size
  with research and policy content added.
