## 6. Message (I2, verbatim — the richest narrative GAU publishes for any of these doctorates)

> The production, analysis, publication, storage and retrieval of information have become crucial
> to the existence of most present day institutions. The need for management personnel who
> understand computer technology and its impact on today's business operations has never been
> greater. The field of management information systems combines computer technology with management
> decision-making methods to analyse, design, implement, and manage computerized information systems
> in an organizational environment.
>
> The Ph.D. program in MIS maintains a supportive teaching and research environment. Students are
> prepared to design and manage computer systems and related-technologies for any organization.
> Courses focus on the analysis of business systems, computer software and hardware concepts,
> network and telecommunication systems, advanced management and advanced research methods.
> Graduates will know how to use system building tools and packages; system simulation tools and
> packages; effectively use current analysis and design techniques for the development of
> information systems; examine the role of new and emerging technologies in areas such as the
> Internet, data communications and programming languages; use project management techniques and
> explore issues relating to the management and development of information systems; put personal
> and professional skills and ideas into practice, and work both individually and in teams. In
> addition to formal course work, objectives of the program are further supported through
> independent study and reading, research projects and thesis research where this research can lead
> to students authoring a paper or co-authoring a paper with one or more faculty members or other
> PhD students. **Students are prepared for careers both in academics and the industry.**

### Extractable published learning outcomes (from the Message, verbatim list)

Graduates will know how to:
1. use system building tools and packages;
2. use system simulation tools and packages;
3. effectively use current analysis and design techniques for the development of information
   systems;
4. examine the role of new and emerging technologies in areas such as the Internet, data
   communications and programming languages;
5. use project management techniques and explore issues relating to the management and development
   of information systems;
6. put personal and professional skills and ideas into practice, and work both individually and in
   teams.

Course focus areas named: analysis of business systems · computer software and hardware concepts ·
network and telecommunication systems · advanced management · advanced research methods.

## 11. Unique selling points supportable from official sources

- An explicitly published, six-point list of what graduates will be able to do — the only GAU
  doctorate in this batch with stated learning outcomes.
- A named artificial intelligence course (ISMA601) at doctoral core level.
- Research co-authorship with faculty members or other PhD students described as a programme
  pathway, not an afterthought.
- Deliberately dual framing: GAU states preparation for careers in both academia and industry.
- Technical core spanning software systems, distributed computing and advanced programming, sitting
  beside advanced management and research methods.
- ECBE citation alongside YÖK and YÖDAK recognition.
