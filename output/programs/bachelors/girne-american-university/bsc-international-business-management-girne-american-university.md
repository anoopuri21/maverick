## 2.1 `short_description`

```
A supplier, customer and colleagues can all be in different countries. GAU's four-year BSc International Business Management connects cross-cultural study with an export and import workshop and supply chain coursework. Teaching is in English; the faculty requires classroom attendance.
```

## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>BSc International Business Management at Girne American University looks at what changes when a business works across countries. The first years cover management, economics, accounting and statistics; the later course grid adds international business, cross-cultural study, an export and import workshop, and supply chain management. The export and import workshop is a course, not a promise of overseas study.</p>
<p>GAU states a four-year, English-taught BSc. Its Business and Economics faculty requires class attendance and describes Moodle as a course support tool, not a replacement for the classroom. The department says the award carries 240 ECTS. Two GAU pages disagree on the separate local-credit total, giving 132 and 120 respectively; neither figure is presented here as settled.</p>

Any applications of the published subjects described on this page are derived coverage, not verified destinations. Tuition and degree-specific international entry conditions are not supplied in the programme panel. Ask GAU to confirm these before you commit.</p>

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**Why do GAU pages give different credit totals?**
<p>The prospective programme description says 132 credits, while the departmental description says 120. The latter also identifies the completed BSc as 240 ECTS, a different credit notation. These are two conflicting local-credit figures, not numbers to average or convert. Request the approved programme specification for your intake from GAU before relying on either local total.</p>

**Can I study International Business Management without attending campus classes?**
<p>Not on the evidence currently published. The Business and Economics faculty describes attendance at traditional classes as compulsory and says Moodle is a support platform. GAU does not advertise a separate online route for this BSc on the reviewed pages. Ask the university which teaching site and attendance schedule would apply to you before making travel or work plans.</p>

**Which subjects make this different from general business management?**
<p>The final-year grid names International Business, Cross Cultural Studies in Org., Workshop in Export and Import, and Supply Chain and Management. Earlier economics, finance and management units build a shared foundation, but those later core titles are specific reasons to consider the international course. Electives such as International Banking are listed separately and should not be treated as compulsory.</p>

**Does the degree lead to a particular job abroad?**
<p>GAU names functions including marketing, human resources, accounting, sales, operations, finance and management consulting. It does not name employers, specific graduate roles or work visas. The possible applications discussed here are a reading of that broad statement and the published courses, not a report of where graduates have been hired.</p>

**What do I need to show for international entry?**
<p>The university's international admissions page explains that application documents depend on the degree. For English-medium departments it also describes a Foundation English School examination and published exemption routes. Neither page supplies an admission score specifically for International Business Management. Have GAU review your qualifications and confirm the applicable language evidence and entry decision directly.</p>

**Where can I check current tuition and scholarship terms?**
<p>The programme panel leaves its fee as a dash, although it indicates that a scholarship may be available. A wider GAU tariff is expressly dated 2025–2026 and cannot establish your present fee or individual award. Request a dated written breakdown covering tuition, any other charges and eligibility for funding before you pay.</p>
