## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Construction projects go wrong in ways that generic project management training rarely prepares anyone for: contracts that shift liability in unexpected directions, supply chains with a dozen weak points, regulators asking harder questions than they did five years ago. Gatehouse has written this diploma around those conditions, describing it as covering the theoretical frameworks and applied strategic approaches needed to manage construction projects within complex, regulated and commercially sensitive contexts.</p>
<p>Six mandatory units carry 20 credits each. They run from strategic leadership and organisational direction, through governance and the legal and commercial frameworks, into change and innovation and sustainability, then integrated planning, delivery and control, followed by professional practice and ethics, and finally research methods. All of them are set in construction, so the case material and the vocabulary belong to the sector.</p>
<p>What makes the specification worth reading is how current it is. Gatehouse names the industry priorities it expects learners to engage with by name: building safety, information management, whole-life value, sustainability, supply chain complexity, and shifting regulatory and commercial practice. This is also the only qualification in its Level 7 range for which the awarding body publishes specific target job titles.</p>
```

2. Work confidently inside the governance, legal and contractual framework of a project
3. Make commercial and financial calls and justify them under scrutiny
4. Plan, deliver and control projects as one integrated system rather than separate functions
5. Lead change and bring innovation into a conservative delivery environment
6. Build sustainability and whole-life value into decisions taken early enough to matter
7. Apply professional ethics when commercial pressure pulls the other way
8. Carry out research that stands up in a construction project management context

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**How is this different from a general project management qualification?**
<p>Context, all the way through. Each of the six units is written for construction: the leadership unit is about organisational direction in construction project management, the planning unit is about integrated construction project planning, delivery and control, and the research unit sits in construction project management too. The specification also names live sector concerns such as building safety, information management and supply chain complexity, which a generic syllabus would not reach.</p>

**What does the assessment actually look like?**
<p>A Portfolio of Evidence, with no examination. You assemble evidence demonstrating that you meet the learning outcomes of each unit, drawing on real project work where you can. Your centre assesses and signs off each unit, an internal quality assurer verifies the judgements, and Gatehouse moderates externally before certification. Resubmission arrangements are covered by the awarding body's published centre policies.</p>

**Which jobs does it lead to?**
<p>Gatehouse names four: senior project manager, programme manager, project director and commercial lead, plus leadership roles within construction organisations and project delivery teams. Those are the awarding body's own words rather than our estimate. It does not publish salary data or employment rates, so we are not offering any. The alternative route it describes is onto a full master's programme in construction or project management.</p>

**Do I need a construction degree?**
<p>Not specifically. The published requirement is an honours degree, or a Level 6 qualification, in any discipline at all. Where an applicant holds neither, Gatehouse permits the centre to admit them individually once readiness for Level 7 study has been established, normally by reviewing a professional portfolio, holding a formal discussion, checking development records and references, and setting a short written task. Hands-on responsibility for delivery carries considerable weight in that assessment.</p>

**How long does it take, and when can I start?**
<p>Gatehouse publishes hours rather than terms. The figures given are 360 guided learning hours set against a total qualification time of 1,200, the balance being your own study. Start dates belong to the delivering centre, not to the awarding body. Since the qualification runs under an exclusivity agreement, the sensible first step is to confirm which centre is approved to offer it and what intake pattern they operate.</p>

**Is it worth anything outside the UK?**
<p>It is a UK-regulated qualification listed on Ofqual's register under QAN 610/7552/8, and Gatehouse approves centres internationally. Recognition by a specific employer, ministry or professional body is their decision rather than something the awarding body can guarantee, so if you need it recognised for a particular licence or a visa route, verify that with the relevant authority before you enrol.</p>
