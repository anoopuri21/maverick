## 3.4 `learning`

A note on this list before you read it. The six outcomes Qualifi publishes for this award do not mention tourism, hospitality, guests, destinations or travel anywhere — they are general business-management statements, and five of the six also appear on unrelated awards in the same range. Two of them are printed run together on one line in the source. We have kept the published outcomes that are genuinely about study skills and reasoning, and derived the subject-specific entries from the four published subject titles. This is coverage drawn from the awarding body's own material, not a tidy restatement of its outcome list.

1. Understand and apply management principles within a working business setting
2. Explore how management theory connects to practice, with employability in mind
3. Analyse problem-solving techniques that belong to business and industry
4. Select, collate, review and analyse information from a wide range of sources
5. Work independently and as a member of a team
6. Take charge of your own development and growth
7. Apply marketing approaches to hospitality and tourism conditions
8. Examine international tourism policy and its effect on destination development

## 3.5 `careers`

Qualifi publishes no job destinations, no salary figures and no employment rates for this qualification. It goes further than that here: the progression panel on its page names no employment route at all. Where most awards in this range list "employment in an associated profession" as an option, this one does not.

The single published route reads, word for word: "Dissertation to complete Master's Degree at UK University".

That sentence needs unpicking, because as written it promises more than it can deliver. This diploma is not a master's degree and does not confer one. No university is named on the page. No partner list, no credit transfer terms and no conditions of any kind are published alongside it. Whether a university will accept the qualification as a basis for a dissertation-only master's, and what it would ask of you first, rests entirely with that university's admissions team. Write to the institution you have in mind, quote the qualification number, and get its position in writing before you pay anyone a fee.

No professional or industry body alignment is published either. The award carries no Institute of Hospitality membership, no travel industry designation and no exemptions from anybody's assessments.

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**Is this really a hospitality qualification?**
<p>Partly. Look at the four subject titles and the balance is clear: tourism is named in all four, hospitality in two, and hospitality is never the sole focus of any subject. There is nothing on rooms division, food and beverage operations, hotel revenue systems or guest service management as standalone topics. If your work is hotel-side and you want depth in hotel operations, ask the centre for the subject descriptors before you commit. If your interest sits in travel, destinations and the policy around them, the weighting works in your favour.</p>

**Four subjects for 120 credits — is that normal?**
<p>It is the shortest list against that credit value in this whole listing; comparable 120-credit awards elsewhere here carry six to eight. There are two innocent explanations. The subjects may simply be unusually large, each worth 30 credits. Or the published web page may be summarising a longer list that appears in full in the centre specification. Qualifi does not publish per-subject credit values, so we cannot tell you which it is. We can tell you it is worth one email to the centre.</p>

**Does it make me eligible for a master's degree?**
<p>Not on its own, and not automatically. Qualifi's page carries exactly one progression line: "Dissertation to complete Master's Degree at UK University". No institution is named in it. No agreement, credit arrangement or entry condition is published behind it. A university receiving your application will make its own judgement about what the diploma is worth and what it still wants from you, and some will want considerably more than a dissertation. Treat the line as a statement of intent, confirm your own position with the university directly, and you will not be caught out.</p>

**Who sets the price and the timetable?**
<p>The approved centre, in both cases. Qualifi accredits and certificates the qualification; it does not teach, does not run cohorts and does not publish a price list. Because no total qualification time is published for this award either, there is no official study-hours figure to hold a proposed schedule against. Put three questions to the centre: when the next group starts, when you would realistically finish, and what the total charge covers once registration and certification are counted in. Two centres can then be compared honestly.</p>

**What is the difference from the Level 5 hospitality and tourism award?**
<p>Size, entry and altitude. At Level 5 the extended award in hospitality and tourism runs to 240 credits, lets people in without a prior qualification, and ranges widely over operational ground. This one is 120 credits, expects a degree or Level 6 qualification before you start, and spends half its subjects on policy and the social dimension of tourism rather than on running a unit day to day. The Level 5 award builds the base; this sits above it and is aimed at people who already have one.</p>

**How current is the published material?**
<p>The centre specification for this qualification carries a January 2021 date, and a separate course brochure linked from the page dates from 2019. We have not opened that brochure and make no claims about what is inside it. Tourism policy and destination development move with government strategy, so the sensible question for the centre is when the taught content was last refreshed — centres revise delivery between specification updates, and the answer tells you how engaged yours is.</p>
