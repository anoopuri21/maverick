## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Choice is the whole point of this version. Qualifi runs 4 IT qualifications at Level 5, and 3 of them fix every subject in advance. This is the one that hands part of the decision to you — 4 compulsory subjects plus 2 of your choosing at the lower stage, then the same arrangement again at the upper stage.</p>
<p>The compulsory spine covers ground nobody in the field can skip. At the 1st stage: information technology and IT ethics, mathematics and statistics for IT, PC maintenance and operating systems, and computer graphics editing with database concepts. At the 2nd: technopreneurship, network security, C#.NET programming, and system administration.</p>
<p>Your 4 choices come from 11 options spanning logical and physical networking, web programming, graphical user interfaces, Java for Android, routing and switching, network design, content management systems, web design, and business-to-business and business-to-consumer e-commerce. 1 rule applies, and Qualifi states it plainly. The award is catalogued as 610/3474/5 and carries 240 credits.</p>
```

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**What is the rule about which options I can combine?**
<p>Qualifi runs 3 specialist versions of this qualification — networking, web design and e-commerce — and it does not want people assembling one of those by stealth. So it forbids the exact option pairs that would add up to a specialist award. Everything else is open. If the combination you wanted turns out to be a forbidden one, that is a signal to enrol on the specialist qualification instead, where the whole programme is built around it.</p>

**Why does the official page mention 120 credits?**
<p>It does, in the overview paragraph, while the specification field on the very same page says 240. We think the 120 figure is left over from the shorter, non-extended version and was never updated. The sister award in this family states 240 explicitly in its own text, which settles it for us. We have used 240 and told you about the inconsistency rather than quietly picking one.</p>

**Can I change my options after starting?**
<p>Qualifi publishes nothing about changing selections, so the answer rests entirely with your centre and depends on timetabling, assessment deadlines and whether the teaching has begun. Ask before you enrol rather than after. It is also worth asking which options the centre actually runs, since offering all 11 requires teaching capacity that not every centre will have.</p>

**Which version should I pick if I know what I want?**
<p>If you already know you want networking, web work or e-commerce, take the specialist award built for it — the whole programme points 1 way and no option decisions are needed. This general version suits someone still exploring, or someone whose work spans several areas and who wants the breadth plus a couple of deeper subjects on top.</p>

**Do I need an IT background to apply?**
<p>Qualifi publishes no prior qualification requirement for this award. What it does set out is an age minimum of 18, English at IELTS 6 or an accepted equivalent for non-native speakers, and the usual 2 references, preferably including an academic referee. The centre then assesses whether you can handle the demands. Given that the compulsory set includes C#.NET programming and network security, some prior exposure will make the 1st term considerably easier.</p>

**Who sets the length and the price?**
<p>Your approved centre, in both cases. The awarding body's role stops at writing the qualification, regulating it and issuing your certificate. It runs no classes, puts no calendar length on this award, publishes no notional hours figure for it, and prices none of its qualifications. Ask the centre for intake dates, expected length and a complete cost, including anything charged for software or examination.</p>
