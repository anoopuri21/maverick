## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Two disciplines share a word and get confused constantly. Occupational safety is about people at work — slips, exposure, protective equipment. Process safety is about the plant: the reaction that runs away, the vessel that over-pressurises, the design decision taken years earlier that nobody revisited. This qualification is firmly the second kind, and the content makes that plain even though the page never spells the distinction out.</p>
<p>Four subjects carry it. Process Safety Management Systems. Risk Based Process Safety. Process Safety Culture and Human Factors. Development as a Strategic Manager. Behind them sit eight published outcomes that name real technique: applying the principles of inherently safer design, analysing the anatomy of process safety incidents using HAZOP — hazard and operability study — and assessing what artificial intelligence and machine learning are changing in the field.</p>
<p>Two things set it apart. It is 60 credits rather than 120, half the size of its sister safety awards. And its entry requirements are the most specialised anywhere here: an engineering degree, or a qualification in chemical, instrumentation, mechanical or a related discipline. Framework number 603/7608/9.</p>
```

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**What is the difference between process safety and occupational safety?**
<p>Occupational safety protects individuals from everyday workplace hazards — falls, manual handling, noise, exposure, protective equipment. Process safety prevents low-frequency, high-consequence events arising from the plant itself: loss of containment, runaway reactions, explosions, structural failure. They need different knowledge and different people. Qualifi does not spell the distinction out on its page, but the content here — management systems, HAZOP, inherently safer design, risk-based process safety — is squarely the second discipline. If you want the first, look at the occupational awards instead.</p>

**Why is this only 60 credits?**
<p>Qualifi publishes the credit equivalency as 60 and offers no explanation, so we can report the figure but not the reasoning. What matters practically is the comparison: both occupational health and safety diplomas in this listing are 120 credits, so this is half the size of either. That makes it a shorter, more focused commitment rather than a lesser one, but you should weigh it accordingly against any requirement an employer has set, particularly if a job specification mentions a credit value or a notional study length.</p>

**What is HAZOP, and what is inherently safer design?**
<p>HAZOP stands for hazard and operability study — a structured, team-based review that works through a plant design or procedure deviation by deviation, asking what happens if a flow is too high, a temperature too low, a valve stuck. Inherently safer design is the principle of removing or reducing a hazard at the design stage rather than adding layers of protection around it later: smaller inventories, milder conditions, less hazardous substances. Both are named in Qualifi's published outcomes. The explanations above are ours; how deeply each is taught is a matter for the specification and your centre.</p>

**Do I need an engineering degree?**
<p>One of the published routes is an engineering bachelor's or master's, and another is a degree or diploma in chemical, instrumentation, mechanical or a relevant process safety discipline. These are the most specific entry requirements of any qualification in this listing, and they tell you plainly who the award is designed for. A fourth bullet mentions three years of work experience, but it is punctuated as a continuation rather than a clear alternative, so whether experience alone is enough is genuinely unclear from the page. Put that question to your centre in writing.</p>

**One outcome mentions Covid. Is the content out of date?**
<p>That outcome does refer to reviewing the impact of Covid on an organisation's process safety culture, and the specification behind the award is dated June 2021, which explains the framing. We are reporting it because it is published, not because it reflects current emphasis. The underlying point — how a major external disruption tests safety culture and competency — has not stopped being relevant. If currency matters to you, ask the centre when the teaching content was last reviewed.</p>

**Timescale and fee?**
<p>Neither appears anywhere on the official page, and no total qualification time is given either, leaving no official hours figure to benchmark against. Both answers come from your approved centre, since the awarding body's role ends at accrediting and certificating the qualification. Given the smaller credit value, ask specifically how long this centre expects a 60-credit programme to run, and make sure the quoted price is itemised rather than a single headline figure.</p>
