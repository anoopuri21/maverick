## 2.1 `short_description`

## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Read the subject list and the scope announces itself. Critical Issues: Supply Chain Crime, Corruption and Terrorism. Critical Issues: Management of Supply Chains during Natural Disasters, Climate Change and Pandemics. Nothing else in this listing names anything close to either. These are not the subjects of a warehousing or procurement course; they are the subjects of a course about failure under pressure.</p>
<p>Qualifi describes the award as being for aspiring strategic leaders in the sector, and says it equips them with the tools to build strategic resilience into their supply chains using a blend of historic models and modern techniques. Resilience is the word that holds the whole thing together. Four further subjects support it: a strategic framework for understanding risk and resilience building, an engineering systems view of supply chain resilience, sector-specific resilience management, and ethical and social supply chain management.</p>
<p>What you will not find is day-to-day logistics. No subject covers warehousing, transport planning, inventory control or procurement. If your worry is whether the chain survives a port closure, a sanctioned supplier or a flooded route, that is the ground it occupies. Credit value: 120 at Level 7, framework number 603/6067/7.</p>
```

## 3.5 `careers`

Qualifi publishes no job destinations, salary bands or employment rates for this award. Three onward options appear on its page and are listed below as written.

1. A Qualifi Level 7 and 8 Diploma — the wording is ambiguous and may mean either or both; Qualifi does not clarify
2. Directly into employment in an associated profession
3. An appropriate dissertation only, for a masters degree at one of Qualifi's university partnerships

That third route needs reading carefully. No partnership is named, no list of institutions is published and no terms are given. This diploma is not a masters degree and does not award one. Whether a university accepts the qualification, what credit it allows and what dissertation it would set are matters for that university alone. Contact the one you have in mind before enrolling.

No professional body alignment is published either. Holding this qualification brings no membership of the Chartered Institute of Logistics and Transport, no CIPS designation and no exemption from any of their assessments.

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**Will this teach me logistics operations?**
<p>No, and it is better to know that now than after enrolling. None of the six published subjects covers warehousing, fleet or transport planning, inventory control, demand forecasting or procurement process. Every one of them is about risk, resilience, disruption or ethics. Qualifi positions the award for aspiring strategic leaders and frames the whole thing around building resilience into a chain. If you need the operational toolkit, this is not where you will find it; if you already have it and keep being caught out by disruption, it is well aimed.</p>

**What is actually in the crime and terrorism subject?**
<p>The subject title is published — Critical Issues: Supply Chain Crime, Corruption and Terrorism — but nothing describing what sits inside it, so what follows is what the title signals rather than a syllabus. It is the only subject anywhere in this listing that addresses deliberate interference with a supply chain, as opposed to accidental failure. Cargo theft, counterfeiting, bribery in procurement and the targeting of logistics infrastructure all sit naturally under that heading. For the real syllabus, ask the centre for the subject descriptor.</p>

**Is this still relevant given the specification dates from 2020?**
<p>The specification does carry a July 2020 date, which is why one subject explicitly names pandemics alongside natural disasters and climate change. Rather than treat that as an expiry notice, judge it on content: deliberate disruption, climate exposure, systemic shock and sector-specific failure have not become less pressing since. What we would suggest is asking the centre when the taught material was last reviewed, since centres update delivery independently of the specification date.</p>

**Can I get in on experience alone?**
<p>Three years of managerial work experience demonstrating current and relevant industry knowledge is published as an entry route, listed alongside a Level 6 qualification in a related sector and a bachelors degree. As with several other awards in this range, the punctuation leaves a little doubt about whether experience stands fully on its own or sits alongside a qualification. No minimum age, English requirement or references are published either. Those are all questions for the approved centre, and worth asking before you apply rather than after.</p>

**How does this compare with the risk management diploma?**
<p>Different kinds of risk. The Risk Management diploma deals with enterprise and financial exposure — governance, strategy risk, risk financing, quantitative modelling. This award deals with physical and operational disruption to the flow of goods, from crime and terrorism to floods and pandemics. They would complement each other, but neither substitutes for the other, and the entry requirements differ as well: that one publishes an age minimum and no qualification requirement, while this one publishes qualification and experience routes and no age limit.</p>

**What will it cost and how long does it run?**
<p>Qualifi answers neither question. It accredits and certificates the qualification; the teaching, the schedule and the price belong to the approved centre you enrol with. No total qualification time is published, so there is no official hours estimate to weigh a proposed timetable against. Before you commit, ask for the intake dates, a realistic end date and a price broken down into tuition, registration and certification.</p>
