## 2.1 `short_description`

```
For people with an idea and no formal grounding to build it on. Three compulsory subjects on innovation, business English and entrepreneurial thinking, then two chosen from planning, marketing and finance. Sixty credits, awarded by Qualifi under Ofqual regulation.
```

## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Qualifi is unusually direct about who this award is for. It was created, in its words, to develop and reward business innovators and entrepreneurs of today and the future, and to bring recognition and professionalism to a wide range of sectors. The emphasis falls on starting and shaping ventures rather than on administering an existing one.</p>
<p>Three subjects are compulsory. Innovation for Business deals with where ideas come from and how organisations handle them. Thinking Entrepreneurially examines the mindset behind acting on an opportunity. English for Business Communication sits alongside them, and its presence as a required subject is deliberate — pitching, negotiating and writing clearly are not optional skills for someone trying to get a venture off the ground.</p>
<p>After that you pick two from three: entrepreneurial business planning, entrepreneurial marketing, or entrepreneurial finance and accounting. That choose-two rule is published explicitly, which makes this the clearest-structured award in Qualifi's Level 3 business range. The qualification carries 60 credits and sits at 603/1051/0 on the Regulated Qualifications Framework.</p>
```

## 3.4 `learning`
1. Read widely around business and enterprise, then form your own view
2. Take knowledge into unfamiliar situations, which is most of what starting something involves
3. Recognise the ethical questions that come with business practice and research
4. See how many disciplines a venture draws on at once
5. Give a clear, credible account of an idea to specialists and to people outside the field
6. Build transferable skills that survive a change of sector or a change of plan
7. Understand how innovation is generated and managed inside an organisation
8. Move on to further study or apply the thinking directly in your own work

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**Will this teach me to start a business?**
<p>It teaches the thinking and the groundwork rather than handing over a template. Innovation for Business and Thinking Entrepreneurially address how opportunities are recognised and acted on, and your two chosen subjects let you go deeper into planning, marketing or the money side. What it cannot do is replace actually trading. Treat it as structure and credibility around what you are doing, not as a substitute for doing it.</p>

**Which two optional subjects should I pick?**
<p>It depends on where you are weakest rather than where you are strongest. If you have an idea but no route to market, entrepreneurial marketing earns its place. If you cannot yet answer how the venture makes money, take the finance and accounting subject. Business planning suits anyone who needs to put a proposal in front of a lender, an investor or a licensing authority. Your centre can advise, and should tell you which it actually runs.</p>

**Why is English a compulsory subject?**
<p>Because communication is most of the job. Qualifi makes English for Business Communication mandatory alongside the two enterprise subjects, which is a reasonable reflection of how founders spend their time: writing proposals, explaining the idea, negotiating terms and dealing with officialdom. It is the only Level 3 business award in Qualifi's range to require it.</p>

**Do I need business qualifications to enrol?**
<p>No. Three starting points are accepted: holding something at Level 2, having worked in the business or enterprise sector and being able to set out where you want to take that, or already holding a Level 3 award in an unrelated subject and wanting to switch into business. An existing venture or trading activity is relevant evidence. Admission runs through the centre, which makes the judgement.</p>

**Is it recognised outside the UK?**
<p>It is a UK qualification regulated by Ofqual and listed on the Register of Regulated Qualifications, and Qualifi states availability as UK and international. Acceptance by any particular employer, bank, university or licensing body remains a judgement those organisations make for themselves, and no awarding organisation can commit them in advance. If you need recognition for a specific purpose, verify it with that organisation first.</p>

**What about timescale and price?**
<p>Qualifi publishes no duration in hours or months for this award and no price at all. Both belong to the centre that delivers the teaching, and both vary considerably between countries and between full-time and part-time study. Ask for a written quotation that separates tuition from registration and certification, so you can compare centres properly.</p>
