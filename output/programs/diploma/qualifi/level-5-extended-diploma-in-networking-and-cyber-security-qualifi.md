## 2.2 `description`

```html
<p>Securing a network and building one are usually taught apart, which is odd, because the people who do either job well tend to understand both. You cannot defend infrastructure you do not understand, and you cannot design infrastructure sensibly without knowing how it gets attacked. This award puts the two together on purpose.</p>
<p>Qualifi is unusually open about how it was assembled. The page names the four existing qualifications its units were drawn from — the Level 4 and Level 5 diplomas in cyber security, and the Level 4 and Level 5 diplomas in IT networking — each with its own number. The result is a combination rather than a new invention, and knowing that tells you precisely what you are signing up for.</p>
<p>Eleven subjects make it up. On the defensive side: threat and risk, network security with data communications, database security and programming, incident response with forensic investigation, cryptography, digital forensics, and security strategy as laws and policy. On the infrastructure side: physical networking, system administration, routing and switching, and network design. Together they come to 240 credits, catalogued as 610/3042/9.</p>
```

## 3.4 `learning`

Qualifi publishes no learning outcomes for this award. The list below describes the subject coverage drawn from its stated aim and published subjects, and should not be read as the awarding body's own outcome statements.

1. Understand how cyber security threat and risk apply to an organisation
2. Understand network security alongside the data communications it protects
3. Work with database security and the programming concepts behind it
4. Follow an incident through response, investigation and forensic examination
5. Understand security strategy as laws, policies and their implementation
6. Build knowledge of physical networking, routing and switching
7. Understand cryptography and the role of system administration in a secure estate
8. Approach network design and administration with security in mind

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**What is the difference between this and the cyber security diploma?**
<p>Think of it as the same security core with a different second half bolted on. Both awards teach the seven defensive subjects. Where the standalone cyber security diploma then turns towards the boardroom, this one turns towards the server room: you study hardware and cabling, run systems, move traffic between networks and design the estate rather than preparing statements and leading a response. The honest test is whether you would rather be in the meeting or at the console.</p>

**Why does Qualifi list other qualification numbers on the page?**
<p>Because it is telling you where the content came from. The overview names four existing Qualifi awards — cyber security at Levels 4 and 5, IT networking at Levels 4 and 5 — and gives each number. This is a merged qualification rather than a newly written one. Most awarding bodies do not disclose that, and it means you can see precisely what the combination contains before enrolling.</p>

**There are no learning outcomes on the official page. Why?**
<p>We noticed, and we are telling you rather than filling the gap. Qualifi's page carries a link to an outcomes section but no outcomes are printed. Since inventing them would mean putting words in the awarding body's mouth, the coverage shown on this page is described from Qualifi's stated aim and its published subject list, and is labelled as such. The Centre Specification should carry the formal outcomes.</p>

**Does it match any Cisco or CompTIA certification?**
<p>Nothing published says so. The content clearly overlaps with what vendor certifications cover — routing, switching, network design, security fundamentals — but overlap is not alignment, and Qualifi makes no claim of mapping, exemption or recognition. If your employer or your target role expects a specific certification, plan to take it separately rather than expecting this to count towards it.</p>

**What does the entry requirement actually ask for?**
<p>Less than you might expect. You need to be 18 or over, meet IELTS 6 or equivalent if English is not your first language, and normally provide two references with one preferably academic. No prior qualification is listed and no experience clause appears. That makes it more open on paper than the cyber security award, which asks for Level 3 qualifications, relevant experience or a Level 4 in another field.</p>

**How much of it is actually networking?**
<p>Four subjects out of eleven sit on the infrastructure side; the remaining seven are security. So the weighting favours security, with enough hardware, administration and design content to keep it grounded in real equipment rather than diagrams. Anyone wanting networking as the main event, with general IT underneath it, will find Qualifi's IT-Networking award the better match.</p>
