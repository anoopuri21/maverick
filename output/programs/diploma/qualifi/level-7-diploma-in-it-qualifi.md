<p>Six required subjects make up the award — Computer Networks, Data Analytics, Database Management Systems, Management Information Systems, Computers and Society, and Computing Projects. ## 3.4 `learning`

This needs saying before the list: Qualifi publishes no learning outcomes for this qualification. The page carries an outcomes link in its own navigation, but the section simply does not exist between the subject list and the entry requirements. Rather than leave the field empty, we have written out the coverage that the six published subjects and Qualifi's own overview imply, and every line below traces back to one or the other. Nothing has been borrowed from the data science or cyber security awards, and nothing from the Level 5 computing qualifications. Treat it as our summary, and ask the centre for the February 2023 specification if you need the awarding body's own wording.

1. Solve complex applied computing problems in an information technology setting
2. Design, plan and organise technology projects that finish on time, to standard and on budget
3. Work with computer networks and the infrastructure that supports them
4. Apply data analytics techniques to practical questions
5. Design, use and maintain database management systems
6. Understand how management information systems support organisational decisions
7. Examine the effect of computing on society and the responsibilities that follow
8. Deliver a substantial computing project from specification through to completion

## 3.5 `careers`

No job destinations, salary bands or employment rates are published by Qualifi for this award, so you will find none quoted here. Two onward options appear on its page.

1. A master's degree in the relevant discipline
2. Employment in an associated profession

The first is written without conditions and without naming an institution. A diploma at this level is not a master's degree and does not award one; whether a university accepts it, and what else it asks for, is settled by that university's admissions office and nobody else. Put the question to them in writing, with the qualification number, before you make plans around it.

One further point, because technology buyers ask it constantly. This is an academic qualification regulated by Ofqual, not an industry certification. No BCS accreditation or membership is published for it, and it carries no Cisco, Microsoft, AWS, CompTIA or Oracle credential, nor any exemption from their examinations. Those are earned separately, through the vendors themselves, and a centre suggesting otherwise is overselling.

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**Why does this page list no official learning outcomes?**
<p>Because Qualifi has not published any for this qualification, and we would rather tell you that than quietly fill the gap. The page has an outcomes link in its navigation that leads nowhere, and the section is missing between the subjects and the entry requirements. What we have published instead is a coverage summary built strictly from the six subject titles and Qualifi's own overview paragraph, labelled as ours. The February 2023 centre specification should contain the formal outcomes, so request it from your centre if you need them for an employer or a university application.</p>

**What does 1,200 hours actually mean?**
<p>It is the total qualification time, an estimate of everything a typical learner spends on the award: time in class or in live sessions, plus reading, assignments, practical work and revision done alone. Nobody is promising that many taught hours, and it is not a schedule. Spread over a year it works out at roughly twenty-three hours a week, every week, which is a useful reality check if you intend to study alongside full-time work. Most awards in this range publish no figure at all, so having one here is genuinely helpful when comparing offers from centres.</p>

**Is the interview difficult to pass?**
<p>Published policy here is an interview at the centre, with experienced managers who hold no formal qualifications eligible for consideration if they show they can handle the demands of the study. It publishes no interview format, no questions and no pass rate, so anyone claiming to know the bar is guessing. Treat it as a conversation about whether your background and available time fit a Level 7 workload. Have your experience, your reasons and your intended schedule ready, and ask the centre what it wants to see beforehand.</p>

**Hours are published — but what about price?**
<p>Price sits with the approved centre, as does the calendar. The awarding body's job stops at designing the qualification, keeping it regulated and printing the certificate at the end. Classes and prices are nothing to do with it. The useful difference here is that the 1,200-hour figure gives you a benchmark the other awards lack: if a centre proposes finishing in four months, you can test that against the published hours and ask how it adds up. Get tuition, registration and certification itemised separately in any quotation.</p>

**Does it overlap with the Level 5 IT qualifications?**
<p>In subject names, yes; in purpose, no. Networks, databases and data appear at both levels because they are the backbone of computing at any stage. The difference is what you are asked to do with them. The Level 5 awards build the foundations over 240 credits with an open way in. This one assumes those foundations, expects a degree, a Level 6 qualification or several years of work behind you, and spends its time on management information systems, societal consequences and delivering a project that holds up.</p>

**Who is the age 20 rule for?**
<p>It is simply the published minimum: applicants must be aged 20 or over. Qualifi gives no reason for the figure, and it is the only award in this listing that uses it — others use 16, 18, 19 or 21, which suggests each was set independently rather than as policy. In practice it rarely bites, since the alternative entry routes already assume a degree or three to four years of work. If you are close to the line, raise it with the centre at the interview stage.</p>
