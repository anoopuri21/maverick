## 3.4 `learning`

These ten outcomes are published by Qualifi and reproduced here in substance, with two small source typos corrected. One thing to notice as you read: they are decidedly managerial. Only the first two sit close to hands-on technical work, and the rest concern planning, command, continuity and leadership.

1. Understand key cryptographic principles and modes
2. Understand the standards, regulations and laws applying to business and government bodies in relation to encryption
3. Understand the core principles of digital investigations
4. Apply the kinds of tool that support professional digital investigations at a strategic level
5. Plan for investigations and forensics teams
6. Understand the physical and human resources needed to manage a major suspected cyber security incident
7. Apply business continuity management to major incident planning and response
8. Understand the role of senior leaders and strategic leadership
9. Evaluate the management streams and performance monitoring that relate to information security
10. Understand how data protection legislation affects strategy-setting and strategic leadership

## 3.5 `careers`

Nothing in the way of destination data, salary figures or employment rates is published for this award, so none is offered here. Two onward routes appear, and the first is unusually specific.

1. A university partner, "where they can complete a further 60 credits to receive a full master's degree"
2. Direct entry into employment in an associated profession

That credit figure deserves attention precisely because it sounds so definite. Qualifi publishes it; we are quoting it, not endorsing it. This diploma is not a master's degree and awards none. No partner university is identified anywhere on the page, no list exists and no terms are set out. Whether a university recognises the 120 credits at all, and how many further credits it would require, is that university's judgement and may be nothing like sixty. Secure a written position from the institution you have in mind before the figure influences anything you pay for.

Separately, and this matters in security hiring: the award is an academic qualification, not an industry certification. No National Cyber Security Centre certification, CREST, CompTIA, (ISC)², ISACA or vendor recognition is published for it, and it grants no exemption from any of their examinations. Those credentials are earned through those bodies directly. Where this qualification can help is in the management and governance side of a security career, which is where its outcomes clearly point.

## 4. `faqs` *(6)*

**The entry requirements mention Level 4 and Level 5 — on a Level 7 award?**
<p>They do, and we have printed them exactly as Qualifi publishes them rather than tidying them up. Every other Level 7 qualification in this listing that names a prior qualification asks for Level 6 or a first degree, so this looks very much like wording carried over from a lower-level template. We are not going to present an unusually low published bar as a feature, and you should not treat it as one either: the study itself is pitched at Level 7, with master's-level reading and writing. Ask your centre what it actually requires, and be honest with yourself about the jump.</p>

**Is this a technical, hands-on course?**
<p>Only in part, and the outcomes show it plainly. Two of the ten deal with cryptographic principles and the regulations around encryption. The other eight concern planning investigations, resourcing a major incident, business continuity, senior leadership, performance monitoring and data protection strategy. That is the work of a security manager, a CISO's deputy or a head of risk, not a penetration tester. If you want to spend your time on tooling and exploitation, look at the operational Level 5 awards or at a practical certification instead.</p>

**Why do the subjects and outcomes not match?**
<p>A fair question, and we cannot reconcile them for you. The outcomes cover digital investigations, forensics teams, incident resourcing, business continuity and strategic leadership, yet none of those has a subject title of its own. Meanwhile Applications of Cyber Security, which is a published subject, has no outcome pointing at it. Either the five published subjects are broader than their names suggest, or the web page is summarising a fuller structure. Both lists are reported here as published; the January 2026 specification is the document that should settle it.</p>

**What about ethical hacking and cloud security?**
<p>Both are named, but in the aims rather than the subject list. Qualifi's published aims say learners will gain in-depth knowledge of network security, digital forensics, ethical hacking and cloud security. None of those four phrases appears in a subject title, so we can confirm the award claims to cover them without being able to show you where. Before enrolling on the strength of any one of them, ask the centre which subject carries it and roughly how much of the assessment it accounts for.</p>

**Duration and price — where are they?**
<p>Not with the awarding body, which publishes neither. Qualifi designs the qualification, keeps it on the register and certificates it at the end; everything about delivery, dates and cost belongs to your approved centre. Because no total qualification time is published, you have no official hours figure to hold a proposed schedule up against — worth remembering if a centre promises a fast completion. Ask for the intake date, the realistic finish date, and a quotation in which tuition, registration and certification are each priced on their own line.</p>

**Will it count towards CISSP, CISM or an NCSC-certified degree?**
<p>Not as published. Qualifi lists no relationship with (ISC)², ISACA, CREST, CompTIA or the National Cyber Security Centre, and no exemption from any of their assessments. Those certifications set their own syllabuses, examinations and experience requirements, and only those bodies can say whether anything you study elsewhere counts. The sensible reading is that this award and a professional certification do different jobs: one evidences regulated academic study in security management, the other evidences tested competence against an industry standard.</p>
