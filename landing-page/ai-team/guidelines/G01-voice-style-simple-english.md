# G01 — Voice & Style: Simple English, Maverick Tone
**Owner:** T7 · **Enforced by:** T8 (writing), T9 (humanize), T10 (final check)

The test for every line: *would a counsellor in Sharjah say this to a family, and would both
the 19-year-old and the 47-year-old nod?* If yes, ship it. If it needs decoding, rewrite it.

---

## 1. Simple English rules

1. **One idea per sentence.** If a sentence carries two thoughts, it becomes two sentences.
2. **Average 12–18 words per sentence.** Some land at five. Evidence sentences may run to
   thirty, but earn every word.
3. **Plain words win:** *start* not *commence*, *fees* not *remuneration structure*,
   *recognised* not *accorded statutory validity*, *call* not *reach out to connect*.
4. **Concrete beats abstract.** Not "local support" but "an office on the 2nd floor of Robot
   Park Tower, Sharjah. Walk in or WhatsApp".
5. **No decoding required.** If a term needs explaining (top-up, equivalency, apostille),
   explain it once, in the same breath, then use it.
6. **Numbers are spoken:** "about AED 35,000 a year" reads better than "AED 35k/yr" in copy;
   tables may stay tight.
7. **Contractions are allowed** where speech uses them: *you'll, we'll, isn't, doesn't*.
   Formal documents (claims, regulation notes) stay uncontracted.

## 2. The Maverick voice

Sharp, well-connected, plainspoken. Confident without chest-beating; a little defiant. The
name allows it. Parent-safe at all times.

**Tone dials (fixed):** Directness 8 · Warmth 6 · Formality 3 · Wit 3 · Urgency 4 (ethical only).

| Do | Don't |
|---|---|
| "No student visa. You stay in your city; the degree comes from the UK." | "Embark on a transformative borderless educational journey." |
| "Campus degrees in Dubai run AED 35k–65k a year. Here's what ours costs, in the same currency." | "We offer affordable and accessible education for all." |
| "A 12-month BBA sounds convenient. The recognition risk is the price." | "Navigate the complex landscape of accelerated credentials." |
| "Sat. classes, weekday recordings, rotational intakes." | "Flexible, dynamic, student-centric learning modalities." |
| "A counsellor calls you back within one business day." | "Our passionate team of dedicated advisors is committed to your success." |

## 3. Two audiences, one page

- The **student** reads for ambition and fit: what will I study, will it fit my job, how fast.
- The **parent** reads for safety and sense: is it real, who awards it, what does it cost,
  who do we call.
- Recognition sections are written parent-first. Programme sections, student-first. Fees and
  FAQ must satisfy both in the same paragraph.

## 4. Sentence rhythm

Short statement. Then the evidence it stands on, which needs the longer line: the source,
the date, the number that makes it true. Short again for the next step.

Vary the openings. Not three sentences in a row beginning "This... This... This..."
Use lists for steps and constraints; use prose for stories and reasons.

## 5. House conventions

- UK spellings: *programme(s), recognised, instalment, counselling, honours*.
- "bachelor's degree" (noun, apostrophe) · "bachelors" attributive, no apostrophe.
- AED first, always. INR only inside a dated parenthetical: (≈ INR 7.7L–14.5L, Sept 2026 rate).
- Cities exact: Dubai · Sharjah · Abu Dhabi. "The Gulf" or "GCC", not "the region".
- Dates: "10 March 2025" in prose; 2025-03-10 in tables.
- No emoji anywhere. No exclamation marks outside a quoted student voice.

## 6. Banned register (instant rewrite)

- Hype: world-class, best-in-class, #1, guaranteed, unbeatable, life-changing, dream come true.
- Vastness: in today's fast-paced world, the ever-evolving landscape of education.
- Empty warmth: we're passionate about, our dedicated family of, journey of transformation.
- Consultant fog: leverage, holistic, seamless, robust framework, paradigm, ecosystem,
  synergies, empower (as a verb of education), unlock (potential, futures, dreams).
- False urgency: limited seats!!! (real intake dates only).

## 7. The one-paragraph standard

Every paragraph must contain at least one of: a fact with a source, a named thing
(university, building, city, route), a number, or a direct instruction. Paragraphs that
contain none of these are transitions. Keep them to a line, or cut them.
